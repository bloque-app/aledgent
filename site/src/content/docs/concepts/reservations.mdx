## Three balance kinds

Every ledger account holds, per asset, three amounts that are read separately:

| Kind | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **available** | Spendable now, with no further decision. The only number a spend is checked against (`REQ-BA-2`) |
| **reserved** | Committed to one identified operation whose outcome is not yet known. Already taken out of available |
| **encumbered** | Restricted by a standing rule on an amount, not tied to one operation: a dispute, a legal hold |

**total** is their sum, and every surface labels it as total. The number a holder is shown and the number a
spend is checked against are the same number, read from the same view (`REQ-BA-3`). Movement between the
kinds is ordinary entries in the book (`REQ-BA-1`). A freeze is not a kind: it is a status of the account,
and changes no balance.

Encumbrance under standing rules is deferred (`US-C5`, `DEF-11`); no V0 command encumbers.

## A reservation is the balance effect, taken at once

A **reservation** moves value from available to reserved for one named operation. It reduces available in
the **same atomic step** as the check that there was enough (`REQ-BA-4`), so two reservations can never
both spend the same value, and a `Customer` account never goes negative (`INV-3`). A reservation whose
balance effect waited would not be a reservation.

`POST /v1/reservations` is acknowledged at `Received` like every command that moves value (`REQ-CS-3`).
Its decision follows: `Reserved`, with available already lower, or `Rejected` with the condition named
(`ERR-InsufficientAvailable`, for one). See [The operation lifecycle](../operation-lifecycle/).

## It ends in a capture or a release

A reservation and its capture or release are **one object, matched by identity** (`REQ-OP-2`,
`INV-14`): the operation reference in the path names the reservation, and what is reserved comes from the
book, never from the caller.

| | What moves | Posting | Ends in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Capture** (`POST /v1/reservations/{reference}/capture`) | `cleared` goes from reserved to the destination account. If `cleared` is less than the reservation, the difference returns to available **in the same posting** | One `Capture` | `Settled` |
| **Release** (`POST /v1/reservations/{reference}/release`) | All of it goes back to available. There is no amount to give | One `Release` | `Released` |

- A capture **above** the reserved amount is `ERR-AmountMismatch`, and nothing changes (`US-C2`).
- A capture and a release are each answered with their decision, and each needs its own client key. A
  repeat under the same key returns the first outcome.
- Once a reservation is captured or released it is closed: a second capture or release of it is refused.
- A bound a grant spent on a reservation that is released, expired or reversed is restored (`REQ-AG-3`).

Patterns for charging by usage: [Metered usage](../../integrate/metered-usage/).

## Deadlines, and why a release can be refused

Every reservation has a deadline: the one its command states, or the deployment's default for
reservations (`REQ-OP-5`). **Past it, the reservation is not released.** It moves to `Needs-Review`, with
its value still reserved, and waits for someone to resolve it: a reservation with no external leg is
released there by one principal. Release it yourself before its deadline if you no longer need it.

A reservation is never released on a timer alone, nor on a request alone (`REQ-OP-9`):

- **With no external leg**, a release on request is safe, and is done.
- **With an external leg** (a withdrawal), every release needs proof that the external instruction can no
  longer take effect: proof of non-inclusion after its expiry, or a proved rejection by the settlement
  layer. Where that proof exists, the reservation is released at once, with no manual step. A release
  asked for without it, while the leg may still execute, is refused with `ERR-ReleaseUnsafe`, and the
  operation moves to `Needs-Review`. That move releases nothing.

<Aside type="caution" title="Trade-off, stated plainly">
Some reservations wait until a human clears them, and holders experience this as stuck money. The
alternative is worse: releasing while the external instruction may still execute is a double spend. The
queue, its owner and its service level are what make "stuck" a bounded condition.
</Aside>

A withdrawal always reserves first: there is exactly one route from intent to the settlement layer, and it
passes through the reservation (`REQ-SO-1`, `INV-13`). See [Assets and the settlement layer](../assets-and-settlement/).

## Reading what is reserved

`GET /v1/balances` returns available, reserved, encumbered and total per asset from one view, and
reserved is the sum of the account's open reservations (`REQ-BA-6`). `GET /v1/operations/{reference}`
returns one reservation's state and deadline.

<Aside type="note" title="Specified, not served by this release">
A query of open reservations, aggregated per (account, asset) and itemised (`REQ-QR-7`), and reservation
reports pushed at a cadence the recipient sets (`REQ-EV-8`), are specified. This release's ingress serves
neither. Until it does, keep your own record of the reservations you made, by operation reference.
</Aside>
