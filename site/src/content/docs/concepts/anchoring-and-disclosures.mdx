## The trade-off behind both

Internal movements never touch the settlement layer, so nobody can rebuild a holder's balance from a public
record. Provability is something aledgent **offers** (to a holder, a verifier, a named counterparty), not
something the world simply has. If aledgent lied and nobody ever asked it for a proof, the settlement layer
alone would not reveal the lie. Anchors and disclosures are how the parties with a stake can ask.

## An anchor

An **anchor** is a commitment to a state of the book, published on the settlement layer. The state it
commits to is a **checkpoint**.

- **When.** Every hour, and on demand (`REQ-PR-1`): when the operator asks, with a justification
  (`POST /v1/ops/anchors`), and when a disclosure concerns entries no final anchor covers yet. In this
  release a disclosure rests on the final checkpoint it names, or the latest final one, and asserts its
  fact as of that checkpoint; a fact newer than the latest final anchor waits for an on-demand anchor to
  become final.
- **What it commits to.** At one consistent cut of the book's writers, every entry up to and including a
  stated sequence, the registry of identities, credentials and grants, the audit trail, and nothing else
  (`REQ-PR-7`, `INV-19`). Taking the cut stops no writer.
- **What it reveals.** Nothing about any account, identity, amount, counterparty or asset (`REQ-PR-2`).
  On the settlement layer an anchor is a number and a 32-byte commitment (`INV-17`).
- **Which construction.** Every checkpoint names the construction of its state commitment, and its anchor
  binds that name, so a recipient knows how to check a proof against it (`REQ-PR-10`). A change of
  construction is a migration whose checkpoint carries both roots, and earlier anchors stay checkable
  (`REQ-PR-11`).
- **Only once final.** aledgent records whether each anchor's publication reached finality on the
  settlement layer. One that has not is never cited as establishing anything (`REQ-PR-8`); citing one is
  `ERR-AnchorNotFinal`.

Every notification carries the latest anchor that was final when its transition was recorded: its number,
the last entry it commits to, and its coordinate on the settlement layer (`REQ-EV-1`). That places each
operation against the anchored history.

## What an anchor proves, and what it does not

<Aside type="caution" title="Non-revision, never solvency">
An anchor establishes **exactly this**: aledgent held a particular book state at a particular time, and it
cannot later claim to have held a different one (`REQ-PR-3`). It establishes **nothing** about whether
that state was correct, whether the value it records exists, or whether the operator is solvent. No
anchoring scheme proves solvency, and aledgent does not claim that anchoring prevents the operator from
cheating. It prevents the operator from *revising* what it committed to.
</Aside>

- **A revision of anchored history is detectable** by anyone holding an earlier anchor, without the
  operator's cooperation (`REQ-PR-4`).
- **Between two anchors, a revision is not detectable this way.** Anchors are at most an hour apart, so
  that hour is the longest such window.
- **Checking an older anchor** needs a reader of the settlement layer that keeps its finalized history, or
  a read saved earlier. Any reader serves the latest.

What answers "does the value exist" is a different check: aledgent compares, continuously, the value it
records as held on the settlement layer with what the settlement layer says it holds, at zero tolerance
(`REQ-PR-5`, `NFR-9`'s third axis). A divergence is an incident. That comparison is the operator's, and
it is aledgent's own report, not a proof to a third party.

Anchoring is never on the critical path of a holder's operation: if it stops, provability is reduced and
nothing else is (`REQ-PR-9`). See [While the settlement layer is away](../settlement-layer-away/).

## A disclosure

A **disclosure** is a statement about ledger facts, addressed to a named recipient. Issuing one
(`POST /v1/disclosures`) needs the subject's authority: its owner, or a grant over it naming disclosure.
The exception is a legal obligation, under which the operator discloses about an account in delegated
custody with a justification, recorded in the audit trail (`REQ-DS-5`). Otherwise it is
`ERR-NotDisclosable`.

It asserts one of four things (`REQ-DS-1`):

| Assertion | Says |
| --- | --- |
| Holds at least | The subject account holds at least an amount of an asset (in this release, of `available`) |
| Occurred | A named operation occurred on the subject |
| Moved | A named operation moved exactly a stated amount of an asset |
| Flows within a bound | The subject's flows in or out over a range of entries meet a stated bound |

Every disclosure names its subject, its recipient (an identity, or an opaque 32-byte reference: never
personal data), its assertion, the checkpoint it rests on, and the deployment's **assurance level**
(`REQ-DS-2`). aledgent refuses to issue one it can tell is false (`REQ-DS-4`, `ERR-DisclosureFalse`).
A disclosure reveals only what it asserts, and gives the recipient no ability to read anything else, now
or later (`REQ-PV-6`).

## What a recipient can check, and what it cannot

<Aside type="caution" title="A disclosure today is aledgent's signature">
In this release every disclosure is a **signed assertion** by aledgent, and the disclosure and the
assurance level both say so (`REQ-PR-6`, `REQ-AR-6`). A signed assertion is evidence of aledgent's
**claim**, not of its **truth**. Disclosures that a recipient can check by a construction, without
relying on aledgent's signature, are deferred (`DEF-3`, `F-023`).
</Aside>

A recipient checks a disclosure without aledgent taking part (`REQ-DS-3`), with the open-source
verifier in the repository (`crates/verify`, the `aledgent-verify` command). Its inputs are the
disclosure, the deployment's attestation key (given, or read from the deployment's record on the
settlement layer) and a public read of the settlement layer. It checks:

1. the disclosure's format and version;
2. aledgent's signatures over it, and that the claim is bound to the checkpoint it names;
3. that the checkpoint's anchor is on the settlement layer, read at a finalized block;
4. that the commitment, recomputed from the checkpoint, is the anchor's.

**A pass means:** aledgent asserted this fact about a book state it committed to publicly and cannot later
deny having held. It does not mean the fact is true, and it says nothing about solvency.

`GET /v1/disclosures/verify` runs the same check at the deployment. It is a convenience, and it is
aledgent answering for itself: a recipient that wants the check to stand without aledgent runs the
verifier.

## The assurance level

A deployment declares, in machine-readable form, which invariants it **enforces** and which it only
**attests**, and every disclosure carries that declaration (`REQ-AR-5`). The first release declares every
disclosure as a signed assertion until an independently checkable construction ships (`REQ-AR-6`).
