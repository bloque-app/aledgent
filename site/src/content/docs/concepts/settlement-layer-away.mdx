Cites `REQ-PR-9`, `AC-J1.4`, `REQ-SL-5`, `REQ-SA-2`, `REQ-SA-7`, `REQ-IN-5`, `REQ-OP-6`, `REQ-OP-9`,
`REQ-SL-4`, `REQ-EV-5`, `REQ-SN-2`, `INV-10`, `INV-11`.

The settlement layer is used for two things only: value entering or leaving aledgent, and anchoring
(`REQ-SL-5`). Internal movements never touch it. So when it, or the settlement adapter that reaches it,
is unavailable, the book goes on; what waits is whatever crosses the boundary. **An anchoring outage
reduces provability and never availability** (`REQ-PR-9`, `AC-J1.4`).

aledgent treats the adapter as unavailable while it declares that it does not serve (it refuses to serve
when it is not connected to the declared settlement layer, `REQ-SA-2`), and once its last declaration has
gone stale.

## What keeps working

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Transfers** between ledger accounts | As always: acknowledged, decided, final on acceptance |
| **Reservations, captures and releases** with no external leg | As always |
| **Issuance and redemption** of any asset | As always, under issuance authority |
| **Reads**: balances, movements, statements, operations | As always |
| **Identities, accounts, credentials, grants, custody** | As always |
| **Notifications** | Delivered as always: they go to recipients, not to the settlement layer, and no operation waits on a recipient (`REQ-EV-5`) |
| **Disclosures** | Issued against the latest **final** checkpoint. A recipient verifies with its own read of the settlement layer, so verification needs the layer to be readable, not aledgent |

## What waits

| | While it is away | When it is back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Anchoring** | The checkpoint in flight stays in flight; anchor lag grows and pages. Notifications go on naming the last final anchor | The checkpoint reaches finality and anchoring resumes |
| **Deposits** | Arrivals are not observed, and nothing is credited: nothing is ever posted for an inbound leg before finality (`INV-10`) | The adapter reads the range it missed. It never advances past a range it did not read; one it was forced to skip is recorded as a gap the operator sees, and reconciled afterwards (`REQ-SA-7`, `REQ-IN-5`) |
| **Withdrawals already submitted** | They stay where they were (`Submitted`, `Observed`, `Final`), with their value reserved. Nothing is failed, released or retried on silence (`REQ-OP-6`) | They go on to `Settled` on finality, or are released at once on a proved rejection, or on proven non-inclusion once their expiry has passed (`REQ-SL-4`, `REQ-OP-9`) |
| **Operations past their deadline** | Move to `Needs-Review`, value still reserved | Resolved on the adapter's proof first, with no human, where it can find one |

## What is refused

| | Refused with |
| --- | --- |
| A new withdrawal, and handing a reserved withdrawal to the adapter | `ERR-SettlementLayerUnavailable` |
| Binding a book-only asset to the settlement layer | `ERR-SettlementLayerUnavailable` |
| A release of a reservation whose external leg may still execute | `ERR-ReleaseUnsafe`, and the operation moves to `Needs-Review`. This is not specific to an outage: it is the rule at all times |

A caller that gets `ERR-SettlementLayerUnavailable` retries the withdrawal later, under the same client key.
Nothing about the account or its balance changed.

<Aside type="note" title="Not an outage: a shortfall">
If the settlement layer is reachable but a position that pays its costs cannot cover what waits on it, the
affected withdrawals **park**. One alert is raised per position, nothing is released, and they resume on
their own once the shortfall clears (`REQ-SN-2`).
</Aside>

## Why it works this way

No caller and no adapter can assert an outcome (`INV-11`): aledgent derives each one from what was
observed. While nothing is observed, nothing is derived, so the safe state is the one the operation is
already in. Releasing a withdrawal's reservation while its instruction might still be included would be a
double spend; crediting a deposit before finality would book value the settlement layer could still
revise.
