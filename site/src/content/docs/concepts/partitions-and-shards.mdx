## One book, held in parts

A deployment has exactly **one book**: one registry, one trial balance, one sequence of anchors. Nothing
splits it by asset, by account kind or by integrating party (`INV-25`). Its state **may** be held in
**partitions** of ledger accounts, so that it scales by adding writers rather than by a faster single one
(`NFR-18`). Holding it in partitions does not split it.

- **Every ledger account belongs to exactly one partition**, fixed by a deterministic function of its
  identifier when it is opened (`REQ-RT-9`).
- **Everything ordered against an account lives in its partition:** its balances in every asset and kind,
  its reservations, and every operation it pays.
- **No identifier changes when the book is re-partitioned.** The partition is part of the identifiers of
  what it holds, so a change in how many partitions there are moves state and renames nothing.
- **The registry, the roles, the trial balance, the anchors and the audit trail are the book's,** not a
  partition's.

Each partition has one durable commit boundary, enclosing a posting's entries, the operation's state
change, its evidence, its notifications and the messages it owes other writers (`REQ-RT-1`).

## What is ordered, and what is not

Every operation that debits an account, or takes, captures or releases a reservation on it, is ordered
against every other such operation on that account (`REQ-RT-10`): their effects are those of some serial
order. Operations on different accounts need no order, and credits to one account may apply in any order.

## A transfer across partitions

When payer and payee are in different partitions (`REQ-RT-11`):

1. **The payer's partition decides and commits the debit.** The value moves from the payer to the book's
   **in-transit position** for the asset, and the credit is enqueued in the same commit. From that commit
   the transfer is **final**: its operation is terminal, and only a new operation undoes it.
2. **The payee's partition applies the credit, exactly once**, within `NFR-19`'s bound (2 s at p99,
   proposed). It cannot be refused: an account that exists can be credited (`INV-9`), and every check the
   transfer needs was made before the debit committed.

The in-transit position is part of the book. The trial balance includes it and is exactly zero at every
commit of every partition (`INV-5`), and reconciliation checks it equals the credits in transit (`NFR-9`).
The payer's view and notification follow the debit; the payee's follow the credit.

<Aside type="caution" title="Trade-off, stated plainly">
For up to `NFR-19`'s bound the value is neither the payer's nor the payee's: it is in transit. "Final"
means final for the payer and for the book; it does not mean the payee can already spend it. When payer
and payee share a partition, both sides commit in one posting.
</Aside>

A balance read for an identity across all its accounts is one query; value moving between two of its own
accounts in different partitions is shown as in transit, never missing or counted twice (`REQ-BA-5`).

## Writers, messages and the consistent cut

The partitions are some of the book's **writers**. The others are the registry of identities, credentials
and grants, the record of settlement instructions and their observations, and the audit trail with its
anchors (`REQ-RT-13`). A writer decides from its own state and the messages it has applied, never by
reading another's. Every message between two writers is enqueued in the sender's commit and applied by the
receiver exactly once and in sequence (`INV-18`).

So there is always a delay between writers, and the specification bounds the ones a holder can feel:

| Delay | Bound |
| --- | --- |
| A credit across partitions | `NFR-19`: 2 s at p99, proposed |
| A revocation reaching every partition | `NFR-20`: 1 s at p99, proposed. Until it arrives, the revoked grant can still be used within its own bounds (`REQ-AU-3`) |
| Everything else | Measured and reported |

An anchor, the trial-balance reconciliation and an asset's binding are each taken at a **consistent cut**:
one commit of each writer, chosen so that nothing applied in it was not sent in it, and nothing in transit
in it was dropped (`REQ-RT-14`, `INV-19`). A cut is decided from what each writer recorded, and taking one
never stops a writer. It trails the newest commits by about one message delay.

## Hot accounts

An account whose debits arrive faster than one partition can order them (an issuer's escrow, a fee
collector, a treasury) may be held as a **pool** of member positions in several partitions (`REQ-RT-12`).
It is still one ledger account, with one owner, one statement and one balance, the sum of its members'. A
debit the pool covers is never refused because no single member covers it. Moving value between members is
an ordinary, audited operation that changes no balance of the account. A holder never sees a member.

## How the plan binds partitions to shards

The plan fixes the partitions and groups them into **shards** (`DEC-22`):

- There are 2¹⁶ partitions. An account's partition is the first two bytes of a BLAKE2b-256 hash of a fixed
  domain and the account's identifier, and account-scoped keys carry it in their top sixteen bits.
- A **shard** is one writer holding a contiguous range of partitions, with a principals writer beside the
  shards. A credit between shards travels in sequence per pair of shards, and each shard holds an
  in-transit position per asset and peer shard.
- **One shard holds every partition** until measurements justify more. With one shard, every transfer
  commits both sides in one commit, and nothing is in transit.

More than one shard, and replicas that can be lost without a restore (`NFR-21`), are the work of `F-031`
and of milestone `M-4`. The number of shards is configuration; the sixteen-bit partition is in the key
layout.
