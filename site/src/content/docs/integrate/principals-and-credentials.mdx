## A principal is an identity with a credential

Everything that calls aledgent is a **principal**: an identity (a person, an organisation, or an agent
acting for one) that holds at least one credential. Every operation records the principal that initiated
it (`REQ-PN-1`).

**Nothing creates the first principal from nothing.** Identities are opened with
`POST /v1/identities`, and only three callers may do that (`REQ-ID-6`):

- the deployment's **operator**;
- the deployment's **identities principal**, the one principal that holds the `open_identity` role;
- the holder of an `open_identity` operator grant (the same role, granted by the operator).

So your service's own identity is opened for you, by the deployment that runs aledgent, with a first
credential you give it. From then on you hold that credential and act as that principal.

## Three credential schemes

An identity holds a **set** of credentials (`REQ-AU-20`). Any credential in the set may act for the
identity, unless it is limited to some of its accounts by `scope`. Each credential has a scheme
(`DEC-19`):

| Scheme | What it is for | Calls the API? |
|---|---|---|
| `api-key` | A service calling aledgent | **Yes**, as `Authorization: Bearer aledgent_ak1.…` |
| `webauthn-es256` | A person, with a passkey | Yes, through a session from a passkey sign-in (`POST /v1/auth/challenge`, then `POST /v1/auth/passkey`) |
| `key` (Schnorr over Ristretto255, the default) | Proving custody: reclaims and recovery approvals | **No** |

<Aside type="caution" title="A key credential does not authenticate requests">
Integrators expect otherwise, so it is said plainly: a `key` credential proves possession only inside the
commands that ask for a proof (a custody reclaim, a recovery approval). It is never a bearer token and
never signs a request. To call the API, a service uses an `api-key` credential.
</Aside>

### api-key

The secret is 32 random bytes, and aledgent keeps only its SHA-256 (`REQ-AU-8`). The key a service
presents has one form:

```
aledgent_ak1.<credential id body>.<base64url of the 32-byte secret, no padding>
```

where the credential id body is the credential's identifier without `did:aledgent:cred:`. There is no key
exchange and no signing: the key is presented as it is. Two ways to get one:

- **The first credential**, given when the identity is opened: you generate the secret and send only its
  SHA-256 as the `verifier`. See [API keys from zero](../api-keys/).
- **Every further key**: `POST /v1/credentials` with `"scheme": "api-key"` and no verifier. Ingress draws
  the secret and returns the whole key once, in `api_key` of the reply. It is never shown again.

`scope` on a credential limits it to some of the identity's accounts. A credential can narrow its own
scope, never widen it (`REQ-AU-4`).

### Passkeys

Passkeys are for people. A person signs in with one and gets a session; a command that moves value under
a session also needs that person's approval of the command. See
[Approving a command](../approving-a-command/).

## Service principals and the `DEP-7` switch

Ingress treats an identity as a **service** only when the deployment's configuration lists it as one.
Every other identity's credential is a **holder** (or agent) credential, and is accepted only where the
deployment sets `allow_holder_credentials` (`DEP-7`). Where it is off, a holder's API key is refused with
`ERR-CredentialInvalid`. Ask the deployment which your identity is.

## You keep the map to your own users

aledgent is its own registry of identities and ledger accounts, and it holds no customer facts
(`REQ-ID-7`). An integrator keeps its own record of which aledgent identity and ledger accounts serve
which of its users. See [A credits integration](../credits-example/#map-your-users-first).
