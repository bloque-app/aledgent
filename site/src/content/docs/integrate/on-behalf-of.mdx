Cites `REQ-PN-1`, `REQ-ID-6`, `REQ-AU-20`, `REQ-AG-1`.

Every command takes an optional header, `on-behalf-of: <identity>`: the identity the caller acts for,
where it is not the caller itself. The operation records both, the initiator and the identity it acted
for (`REQ-PN-1`).

## Who sends it

**The identities principal, acting for an identity it manages.** That is the case it exists for
(`REQ-ID-6`, `REQ-AU-20`). An identity it manages is one that is not the operator, not an agent, and holds
no account in owned custody. Acting for it, the identities principal may:

- **register a credential into that identity's set**: `POST /v1/credentials` with
  `on-behalf-of: <identity>` (a new API key for a service that lost its own, for example). The identities
  principal must call with an **unscoped** credential, and the new credential's `marker` must be
  `ordinary`: a holder-held recovery credential is never added this way. Otherwise the call is
  `ERR-NotAuthorised`. A credential that proves possession, such as a passkey, is still proven by its own
  ceremony;
- grant delegated authority over that identity's accounts in delegated custody.

Each such action is attributed to the identities principal's authority and to the identity it acted for
(`REQ-AU-7`).

## Who does not

- **A grantee never sends it.** A service charging a user's account calls as itself; its grant over the
  account is its authority. See [Which key do I call with?](../which-key/).
- **An agent does not need to.** An agent identity always acts for the identity it was opened for
  (`acts_for`, `REQ-AG-1`). It may send that same identity in `on-behalf-of`, or nothing; any other
  identity is refused with `ERR-CredentialInvalid`.

## Example: replacing a lost key

```sh
curl -X POST "$INGRESS/v1/credentials" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $IDENTITIES_PRINCIPAL_KEY" \
  -H "on-behalf-of: did:aledgent:id:<the service>" \
  -H "client-key: reissue-key-<the service>-2026-11-20" \
  -H "content-type: application/json" \
  -d '{"marker":"ordinary","scheme":"api-key"}'
```

The reply's `api_key` is the new key, shown once. Hand it to the service through your secret store, then
have the service (or the identities principal, the same way) remove the lost credential.
