Cites `REQ-ID-6`, `REQ-ID-7`, `REQ-AS-3`, `REQ-AS-7`, `REQ-AU-10`, `REQ-OP-10`, `REQ-PO-5`, `REQ-CS-7`,
`INV-14`.

A worked example: a platform sells credits through a payment provider, keeps them in aledgent, and charges
them as its users run tasks. The credits are a **book-only asset** (`REQ-AS-7`): value enters only by
issuance and leaves only by redemption. Below, `CRD` is that asset; amounts are minor units, as strings.

## Setup (once, with the deployment's operator)

<Steps>

1. **The asset.** The operator registers `CRD`, book-only, with its one precision. aledgent normalises
   asset ids to uppercase on every route (`REQ-AS-3`), and replies carry that form: keep `CRD` in your
   own records, so your comparisons match what aledgent returns.

2. **Your service's identity and key.** The deployment opens your service's identity with an API key; see
   [API keys from zero](../api-keys/).

3. **Issuance authority.** The operator grants your service an issuance grant over `CRD`, naming `issue`
   and `redeem`, with a `per_window` bound and `assets` naming `CRD` (`REQ-AU-10`).

4. **Your collection account.** Your service opens its own account, where captured credits land:

   ```sh
   curl -X POST "$INGRESS/v1/accounts" -H "Authorization: Bearer $API_KEY" \
     -H "client-key: collection-account" -H "content-type: application/json" \
     -d '{"owner":"'$SERVICE_IDENTITY'","kind":"customer","custody":"delegated"}'
   ```

   `result.account` is its identifier.

</Steps>

## Map your users first

aledgent does not know your users and holds no facts about them (`REQ-ID-7`). You keep the map from your
user to the aledgent identity and ledger account that serve them, in your own store, or you use the
deployment's own resolver if it offers one.

That map is a privilege: whoever can read it can find any user's account. Prefer the design where the
**deployment's identity service** opens the identity and the account, and hands your service only the
account identifier and a grant over it. Then your service never resolves users it was not given.

## Signup: open the user's account

Two ways, depending on the deployment:

- **The identities principal opens it** (the recommended design above). It opens the user's identity and a
  customer account in delegated custody, and grants your service a delegated grant over that account
  (`REQ-ID-6`). You receive the account id.
- **You open it under an `open_account` grant** over the user's identity, made by the identity or by the
  identities principal; see [Grants](../grants/#opening-accounts-for-an-identity).

Either way, the grant your service holds over the user's account names `reserve`, `capture` and
`release` (and `transfer`, for [costs known afterwards](../metered-usage/#the-cost-is-known-only-afterwards)),
limited to `CRD` and to your collection account as counterparty, with bounds.

## Payment: credit on the provider's paid event

When the provider reports a paid invoice, issue the credits to the user's account. **Use the provider's
invoice id as the client key**, so a webhook delivered twice never credits twice (`REQ-OP-10`), and put the
provider's ids in `reference` for reconciliation (`REQ-PO-5`):

```sh
curl -X POST "$INGRESS/v1/issuances" -H "Authorization: Bearer $API_KEY" \
  -H "client-key: invoice-$INVOICE_ID" -H "content-type: application/json" \
  -d '{"asset":"CRD","destination":"'$USER_ACCOUNT'","amount":"5000",
       "reference":{"invoice":"'$INVOICE_ID'","customer":"'$PROVIDER_CUSTOMER_ID'"}}'
```

An issuance is answered with its decision. A `503 ERR-DecisionUnavailable` means "retry with the same key",
not "failed" (`REQ-CS-7`): answer the provider's webhook with an error so it retries, or retry yourself.
See [While the core is away](../core-away/).

## Usage: reserve, then capture or release

For each task: reserve its cost against the user's account, run it, then capture the whole reservation
into your collection account or release it. Use the task id in the client keys. A capture may clear less
than its reservation, and the rest returns to the user, but never more (`INV-14`); the patterns for
variable costs are in [Metered usage](../metered-usage/).

A reservation is acknowledged at `202 Received`. Act only on its decision: `Reserved` (run the task) or
`Rejected` with `ERR-InsufficientAvailable` (the user has too few credits).

## Burning: redeem from your collection account

Captured credits sit in your collection account. Redeem them when you account for them, from that account:

```sh
curl -X POST "$INGRESS/v1/redemptions" -H "Authorization: Bearer $API_KEY" \
  -H "client-key: burn-2026-11-20" -H "content-type: application/json" \
  -d '{"asset":"CRD","source":"'$COLLECTION_ACCOUNT'","amount":"120000"}'
```

A redemption needs both your issuance grant and authority over the source account, which you have as its
owner. There is no "redeem a reservation": a redemption is a separate command from an account.

## Refunds

- **A charge made in error:** transfer it back from your collection account to the user's account.
- **Unused credits, refunded in money:** move them out of the user's account to your collection account
  with a `transfer` (your grant must name `transfer`), redeem them, and then refund on the provider, with
  the provider's refund id in each client key and `reference`.

## Reads

| You want | Read |
|---|---|
| A user's credits | `GET /v1/balances?account=<user's account>&asset=CRD`: `available` is what can be spent, `reserved` is held by open reservations |
| What happened to one command | `GET /v1/operations/{operation_reference}` |
| A user's history | `GET /v1/movements`, filtered and paged; see [Reading the book](../../start/reading-the-book/) |

## Common errors

| You see | Usually | Do |
|---|---|---|
| `ERR-NotAuthorised` | The grant does not name this operation, asset or counterparty, or has lapsed | Check the grant ([Grants](../grants/)) |
| `ERR-UnknownAsset` | The asset is not registered, or the id is not 2 to 12 letters and digits | Check `GET /v1/assets` |
| `ERR-AmountMismatch` | A capture that is not the full reservation | [Metered usage](../metered-usage/) |
| `ERR-InsufficientAvailable` | The user's available credits are short | Decline the task |
| `ERR-ClientKeyReused` | A key reused for a different request | One key per request, derived from your own ids |
| `ERR-DecisionUnavailable` | No core decided in time | Retry with the same key after `Retry-After` |
| `ERR-CredentialInvalid` | The API key | [API keys from zero](../api-keys/) |

Every condition is in the [error catalogue](../../errors/).
