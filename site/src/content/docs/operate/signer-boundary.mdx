Cites `NFR-8`, `REQ-SA-8`, `REQ-AU-8`, `F-035`, `F-042`.

Key material never lives in the process that holds the book (`NFR-8`, `REQ-SA-8`). Every key the operator
holds is in **`aledgent-signer`**, a process of its own that holds no book state (`F-035`).

## What it holds and who reaches it

- **Its keys**: the core's key ring, the clerk key that signs anchors and disclosures, the Agent's keys,
  ingress's feed key, the settlement adapter's lane keys. Each is read from its source at start: a
  secret store's key-value field, or a key held by an engine that never lets it leave (Vault Transit,
  `F-042`; a cloud KMS, opted in per key). A key file is for development only.
- **Its clients**: the core host, ingress, the settlement adapter. Each reaches the signer over a Unix
  socket, as its own user id, with **its own token**, and may use only the keys granted to it. A
  revocation takes effect on the client's next request.

The book's process asks the signer to sign; it never sees a key, and a key is never readable from any
process's environment.

## What it is not

- It does not hold bearer tokens for ingress, such as [the operator token](../operator-token/). Those
  authenticate callers; they are not keys.
- It is not a policy engine. Whether a command may happen is decided by the book; the signer signs what
  its granted clients ask of the keys they are granted.

## Configuration, in outline

`deploy/templates/signer.json` in the repository is the shape:

- `listen`: the socket, and `socket_mode`;
- `clients`: per client, its user ids, the SHA-256 of its token (`aledgent-signer token-sha256 <file>`),
  and the keys it may use;
- `keys`: per key, its id, scheme and source.

Rotating a client's token is two entries for a while: add the new hash, `kill -HUP` the signer, switch the
client, remove the old hash.
