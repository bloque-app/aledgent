Cites `REQ-RC-7`, `REQ-AU-2`, `REQ-ID-7`, `REQ-AT-1`, `REQ-AT-3`, `INV-16`, `INV-17`, `REQ-LA-4`,
`ERR-UnboundedGrant`.

## What it is for, and when to run it

Run it when a service of yours needs an account of its own that is not a customer's: a fee account, a
provider-cost account, a float. Suggested owner: platform, with the owner of each service that holds a grant on
the account.

**Rules that do not bend:** a system account is owned by **the operator's identity** and opened under it;
services act on it only through **account-scoped, bounded grants** on exactly the accounts they operate; no
service holds the operator's token or credential. Everything goes through the command surface (`REQ-AT-3`):
nothing writes a store by hand, and nothing moves value outside a command. A service names a system account by
its **role**, never by its identifier: the identifier changes at every genesis, the role does not.

The accounts that exist at genesis come from the chainspec with their role aliases already; this runbook is for
one created after the book is serving.

## Preconditions

- The operator's credential, used for steps 2 to 4 only. Each of those needs a live operator grant of the
  matching kind, for as short a time as it takes ([Least privilege](../../least-privilege/)).
- Your ingress URL, and the service principal's identity (`did:aledgent:id:…`).
- Below, `$OP` is the operator's bearer token ([The operator token](../../operator-token/)), `$ACCOUNT` the new
  account, `$SERVICE` the service principal and `$INGRESS` ingress's URL.

## Steps

### 1. Decide, and record the decision

In your change's tracking issue, before anything is opened:

- the **role**: `<service>/<purpose>`, for example `payments/provider-cost/example`. Lower case, `/` separated,
  at most 256 bytes, no personal data (`INV-17`);
- the **kind**: `customer` (or `suspense`) unless it must go below zero, then `treasury` with a per-asset limit;
- the **assets** it holds;
- **which service principal** acts on it, with which operations (`transfer`, `reserve`, `capture`, `release`),
  and which bounds (per operation, per window, assets, counterparties). A role with no service stays under the
  operator alone and gets no grant.

The issue records the decision, never the identifiers.

### 2. Open the account, as the operator, under the operator's own identity

```sh
curl -sS -X POST "$INGRESS/v1/accounts" -H "authorization: Bearer $OP" \
  -H "client-key: system-payments-provider-cost-example" -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"owner":"<the operator identity>","kind":"customer","custody":"delegated"}'
```

The operator identity is what `aledgent-ingress founded --config <file>` prints. The reply's `result.account` is
`$ACCOUNT`. The client key makes a retry safe: the same key returns the same account.

### 3. Register its role

```sh
curl -sS -X POST "$INGRESS/v1/ops/accounts/$ACCOUNT/aliases" -H "authorization: Bearer $OP" \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"source":"system-role","name":"payments/provider-cost/example","justification":"<issue>: <why>"}'
```

`source` is `system-role` for every role: the same source a seeded genesis gives its system accounts (16 bytes,
`system-role` zero-padded; its hex spelling `73797374656d2d726f6c650000000000` is the same source). An alias
names one account **for good**: a role already registered (to this account or another) is refused
`403 ERR-NotAuthorised`, and nothing re-points or removes it. Pick the name in step 1 with that in mind. The
command is audited with its justification (`REQ-AT-1`).

### 4. Grant the service what it needs

If the service principal is not tied to its integrating party yet, tie it first
(`POST /v1/ops/integrating-parties/{party}/principals`). Then the account-scoped grant, from the account's owner
(the operator) to the service, with the bounds of step 1:

```sh
curl -sS -X POST "$INGRESS/v1/grants" -H "authorization: Bearer $OP" -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"kind":"delegated","principal":"'"$SERVICE"'","subject":{"account":"'"$ACCOUNT"'"},
       "ops":["transfer"],"per_window":{"length":86400000,"limit":"<minor units>"},"assets":["<asset>"]}'
```

A grant needs at least one bound (`REQ-AU-2`, `ERR-UnboundedGrant`). This account-scoped grant is the **only**
way a service reaches a system account: an operator grant over every delegated account (such as a custody
service's) never covers an account the operator's identity owns, whatever its custody. So a system account is
safe in delegated custody, and no broad grant is ever the answer for one ([Grants](../../../integrate/grants/)).

### 5. Make the service name the role, not the identifier

One line in the service's configuration names the role. At start the service resolves it as its own principal:

```sh
curl -sS "$INGRESS/v1/aliases?source=system-role&name=payments/provider-cost/example" \
  -H "authorization: Bearer <the service's token>"
# {"account":"did:aledgent:acct:…"}
```

It answers only to a principal that reaches the account: before step 4 the service gets `404 ERR-UnknownAccount`,
as for a role that does not exist. Never paste the account's identifier into a configuration, a secret store's
entry or an environment variable.

## Verify

All reads; nothing changes.

- **The grants on the account** are exactly the decision's: one per service of step 1, nothing else.

  ```sh
  curl -sS "$INGRESS/v1/accounts/$ACCOUNT/grants" -H "authorization: Bearer $OP"
  ```

  Each item names the grant, its grantor (the operator), when it was granted, `revoked` if it was, and its terms
  (holder, operations, bounds). Only the operator and the account's owner read this; a grantee gets `403`.
- **The role resolves**, as the service (step 5), to `$ACCOUNT`.
- **The account** is empty and owned by the operator: `GET /v1/balances?account=$ACCOUNT` shows zero.
- **One smoke operation** as the service inside its bounds succeeds, and one outside them is refused
  `ERR-BoundExceeded` or `ERR-NotAuthorised`.
- **No other service reaches it**: one operation on it with a token that holds only a broad grant is refused
  `ERR-UnknownAccount`.
- **The audit trail** (`REQ-AT-1`) holds the open, the alias and the grant, each attributed.

## Roll back

Retiring the account is the roll back. Revoke the grant (`POST /v1/grants/{grant}/revoke`), let the service stop
resolving the role, drain the account through the command surface, freeze it
(`POST /v1/ops/accounts/{account}/freeze`) and close it (`POST /v1/accounts/{account}/close`; only at zero
balance, `REQ-LA-4`). The alias stays, as every alias does, and keeps naming the closed account: a role is never
reused for another account. Choose a new role for a replacement.

### Archived identifiers

An identifier a migration left behind (the exclusion list the chainspec commits) is answered
`410 ERR-IdentifierArchived` wherever a request names it, for good: in a path, a query parameter, a command's
body, or as an alias's name. It cannot become a role. To use such an identity again, open it afresh with a new
identifier.
