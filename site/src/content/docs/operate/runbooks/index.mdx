Cites `NFR-9`, `NFR-11`, `REQ-AT-3`, `REQ-OS-1`, `F-018`, `F-039`.

A runbook is the page you open when an alert fires, or before you do something that is hard to undo. Each
one says what it is for and when to run it, what must be true first, the steps, how to check that it
worked, and how to go back. Each cites the requirements, invariants and features it serves, so you can read
what the system promises before you act on it.

The pages name no deployment. Wherever a step needs infrastructure, it says what kind (your secret store,
your object store with Object Lock, your database server, your container scheduler), and you substitute
your own. Names that look like `AledgentCoreHalted` are alerts in the rules shipped under `ops/prometheus/`
in the repository; names like `aledgent_core_halted` are the metrics `opsctl exporter` publishes.

## Rules that hold on every page

- **Nothing edits a store.** Not the book, not the received-log, not the audit store, not the archive
  (`REQ-AT-3`). A restore writes only into an empty directory or database. A correction to the book is an
  operation through the command surface, with its justification on the audit trail.
- **An operator command needs a live grant of its kind, and a justification.** The operator holds no
  capability by default: grant yourself the one kind, for one task, then revoke it
  ([Least privilege](../least-privilege/)). A command without a justification is refused
  (`ERR-JustificationRequired`).
- **A secret never appears on a command line, in a log, in a URL or in a repository.** Secrets go to a
  process through its environment or a file only that process can read, from your secret store
  ([The signer boundary](../signer-boundary/)).
- **Divergence and key exposure are incidents, not tickets** (`NFR-9`). Open yours, and freeze the
  evidence before you change anything.
- **Operations that need a human are resolved with evidence, never released on a timer**
  (`REQ-OP-9`; [Overview](../overview/)).

<Aside type="note" title="What the examples assume">
Examples call the operator API with `curl`, `$INGRESS` for ingress's URL and `$OP` for the operator's
bearer token ([The operator token](../operator-token/)). Commands marked `opsctl`, `aledgent-settle`,
`aledgent-signer`, `aledgent-verify` and `aledgent-core-host` are the binaries the repository builds; the
core image carries `opsctl` beside `aledgent-core-host`.
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Restarting the core host">
A core host stopped cleanly (SIGTERM or SIGINT within the stop timeout) keeps its replica and reopens it in a
fraction of a second. Anything else (a crash, SIGKILL, a stop that times out, a halted core, a fresh node with
no persistent volume) rebuilds the replica from genesis, in time proportional to the book's history. Several
runbooks below end in a restart; read [Core restarts and host rolls](../core-restarts/) first, and plan the
window from your own archive's replay time.
</Aside>

## Alerts without a page of their own

| Alert | What to do |
|---|---|
| `AledgentReviewOverdue` | An operation in the review queue is past its service level. Find the operations in the review queue (`Needs-Review`), and resolve each with evidence: `POST /v1/ops/operations/{reference}/resolve`, with a `target` (`settle` or `release`), the `evidence` (a settlement record, non-inclusion under the last finalised head, or, last, `no_proof` with a second principal agreeing) and a `justification` ([Overview](../overview/)). Resolve none without evidence (`REQ-OP-9`) |
| `AledgentAdapterSignerNotReady` | A settlement key's signer is not ready; nothing is signed. See [Settlement adapter outage](./settlement-adapter-outage/), step 7 |
| `AledgentCoreBackupLate`, `AledgentAuditBackupLate`, `AledgentIngressBackupLate`, `AledgentAdapterBackupLate` | No verified backup for that store within its schedule. `opsctl backup due --root <dir>` says which are due; take them (`opsctl backup pg`, `opsctl backup audit`; the Agent core's backup is its archive, [The core's off-node archive](./core-archive/)) and check each with `opsctl backup verify` |
| `AledgentAuditGap` | The audit store's head does not match what the core emitted. Treat it as the refused case in [The audit trail does not ship](./audit-shipping/) |
| `AledgentInternalFault` | A command failed on a fault inside aledgent and was answered `ERR-Internal`. Keep the log lines and the audit record, reproduce off production as in [The core stalls or halts](./core-halt/), and fix forward; do not retry in a loop |
| `AledgentUndeliveredNotification` | A notification has been owed for more than fifteen minutes. Check that the recipient's endpoint answers, and that its secret is registered (`aledgent-ingress recipient`); the notification is delivered when it does |

## Who owns what

Pages suggest an owning role: **platform** (hosts, volumes, images, databases), **security** (keys,
integrity incidents), **settlement operations** (the adapter and the layer), **treasury** (balances that
fund the layer) and **finance operations** (reconciliation). Your organisation may split them differently;
what matters is that each page has one named owner on the day.

## What is not here

Deployment guides (a cloud guide and a one-machine guide) and the upgrades page are separate pieces of the
operator guide. Runbooks that depend on one organisation's own release process or cutover plan are not
part of the public set.
