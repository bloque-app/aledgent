Cites `REQ-AT-1`, `REQ-AT-2`, `REQ-AT-3`, `NFR-6`, `NFR-11`.

## What it is for, and when to run it

Run it when any of these page: `AledgentAuditNotShipped`, `AledgentAuditShipLag`, `AledgentAuditStoreDown`,
`AledgentAuditStoreRefused`, `AledgentAuditBackpressure`. They are routed at once and every 15 minutes.
Suggested owner: security, with platform for the store's volume.

How shipping works:

1. The core emits every audit record inside its commit, into its audit outbox.
2. A shipper thread in the core's process (`aledgent-core-host serve`, or ingress's in-process core) copies
   the outbox to the audit store in batches, outside the core's locks, using the store's own writer
   credential (`ALEDGENT_AUDIT_WRITER_CREDENTIAL`).
3. The store takes each record **once, in order**: only the next sequence number, linked to the one before.
   A record it already holds is accepted again and appends nothing; any other is refused.
4. Records leave the outbox only once the store confirmed the append. Nothing is lost: every record can be
   derived again from the archive.
5. **Back-pressure.** At `max_unshipped` records waiting (65 536 by default, about 23 MiB), the core refuses
   new inputs. They stay `Received` in ingress's log, in order, and decide once the store takes records
   again. Nothing decides without its record, and nothing is dropped.

| Alert | Means |
|---|---|
| `AledgentAuditNotShipped` | No `audit_store` in the configuration: nothing ships, the outbox grows with the book |
| `AledgentAuditStoreDown` | The last rounds did not reach the store: the volume, its mount, its permissions or the credential |
| `AledgentAuditShipLag` | A record has waited over five minutes: the store is down, or slower than the core |
| `AledgentAuditBackpressure` | The outbox is at its bound: the core decides nothing until the store takes records |
| `AledgentAuditStoreRefused` | The store refused the core's record, or holds another at its head. Shipping has stopped. An integrity incident |

## Preconditions

- Read the signals: `aledgent_audit_unshipped_records`, `aledgent_audit_ship_lag_ms`,
  `aledgent_audit_store_up`, `aledgent_audit_store_refused`, `aledgent_audit_backpressure`.
- Read the core's standard error for `the audit store does not take records` or an `aledgent-core: ALERT`
  line naming the record.
- Tell the cases apart: **not configured**, **unreachable** (store up is 0, not refused), or **refused**.

## Steps

1. **Not configured.** Add `audit_store` to the deployment configuration that the core reads (the same file
   ingress reads; `aledgent-core-host serve` reads it too): `{"dir": "<the audit volume's mount>"}`.
   Provision `ALEDGENT_AUDIT_WRITER_CREDENTIAL` from your secret store (16 bytes or more). The directory must
   not be inside the core's `--state`; the store refuses that. Restart the core. An empty directory is
   initialised on the first start, and the first rounds ship the whole trail.
2. **Unreachable.** Restore the store's volume: its mount, free space and permissions for the core's user. Do
   not move or edit the store's files. The shipper retries every 500 ms by itself, so no restart is needed.
   If back-pressure has started, decisions resume as soon as the outbox drains under its bound.
3. **Refused.** Do not restart in a loop, and do not repair the store: it is evidence (`REQ-AT-3`).
   - Capture the `ALERT` line. It names the record number and the store's refusal (`Rewrite`, `Gap`,
     `ChainBreak`, or the head that differs).
   - The usual causes: the store belongs to another book (a reused volume); the core was restored from an
     older backup while the store kept going, so the store holds records of inputs the restored core no longer
     has; or someone wrote to the store.
   - After a restore of the core ([Total-loss recovery](../total-loss-recovery/)) the store is ahead of the
     restored book. Keep it aside, read-only, as evidence of the decisions the restore lost. Point
     `audit_store.dir` at a store restored from a backup at or before the core's restore point, and restart.
   - Treat it as a security incident at your highest severity. The security owner decides with the evidence;
     the core's trail and the store's are compared with `opsctl` reads, never by editing either.
   - While refused, the outbox grows to its bound, then the core holds every input. That is by design: no
     decision without its record kept apart.
4. **Back-pressure that will last.** The bound is configuration (`audit_store.max_unshipped`). Raise it, with
   a restart, only if the store will return. Each record holds about 350 bytes of the core's memory, so check
   the headroom first.

## Verify

- `aledgent_audit_store_up == 1`, `aledgent_audit_store_refused == 0`, `aledgent_audit_backpressure == 0`.
- `aledgent_audit_unshipped_records` falls to a few, and `aledgent_audit_ship_lag_ms` to under a second.
  `aledgent_audit_shipped_records` rises with the book.
- `opsctl backup audit --store-dir <dir> --root <backup root> --id <id>` takes a verified copy of the store;
  it re-reads every segment and walks the chain.
- If inputs were held: the oldest undecided row falls under the decision bound, and nothing is still
  `Received` after the drain.

## Roll back

Nothing here changes the book. A configuration added in step 1 can be removed again with a restart. Records
already shipped stay in the store, which never removes a record.
